Arms-Related Data and Early-Warning Portal

Incorporating arms-related data into early-warning systems

A one-stop shop with information, tools, and resources concerning arms-related data and early-warning systems.

Intended users

Early-warning providers

Organizations and practitioners collecting, monitoring and analysing information to identify emerging conflict risks.

Arms data collectors and analysts

People working with arms-related information, datasets, flows, incidents and indicators.

Policymakers, practitioners and researchers

Working in conflict prevention, armed violence reduction, early warning or arms control.

Background

In 2025, more armed conflicts were waged than in any year since the Second World War. Armed conflicts are also intensifying, lasting longer, and growing more intractable.

The time to act is before conflict breaks out. Prevention not only reduces human suffering—it also limits the financial cost.

One way to prevent armed conflict is to tackle an essential ingredient: weapons proliferation and misuse. Without weapons there can be no armed conflict. With them, fighting appears to break out more easily, last longer, or intensify, depending on the context.

Crucially, information about weapons can warn of conflict on the horizon. Rising prices on illicit arms markets may indicate greater demand and a heightened risk of fighting. The appearance of new weapons or tactics may signal an escalation in violence. A decrease in seizures may suggest that fewer illicit weapons are circulating—or that the government has lost control on the ground. Interpreted in context and alongside other indicators, arms-related data can sharpen conflict analysis, risk assessments, and forecasting.

Conflict early-warning systems play a vital role in effective conflict prevention by identifying risks and alerting those able to respond. They are only as good as the data on which they rely. However, few systems systematically collect, monitor, or analyze information concerning the proliferation, access, use and management of weapons (arms-related data).

In 2024, Conflict Armament Research, the Peace Research Institute Oslo, Uppsala University, and UNIDIR brought together leading experts in arms control and in early warning, two communities that have historically had little contact. Together, they charted practical paths forward to strengthen the use of that data in early warning.

This Portal is part of those efforts. It aims to provide practitioners with the information, tools, and resources to incorporate arms-related data into early-warning systems.

UN decisions supporting this work

Report of the Secretary-General: Small arms and light weapons (2025)

Paragraph 8. To integrate arms control into prevention efforts, the Secretary-General further encourages Member States to strengthen national capacities for collecting, analysing and sharing arms and ammunition flows data as part of their conflict prevention and early warning strategies. This includes investing in interoperable data systems, adopting standardized indicators and fostering collaboration between disarmament, law enforcement and early warning institutions.

Experts and Member States can utilize tools such as the Arms Flows and Early Warning Dashboard as a working tool to foster cross-thematic knowledge exchange and conduct predictive analysis.

UN Resolution A/RES/79/1

We decide to […]:

  • [Deploy] all the knowledge, resources and capacities at our disposal, fulfilling our obligations under international humanitarian law, including by […] strengthening early warning [Action 15(d)];
  • Address the risks associated with illicit trade in small arms and light weapons, their parts and ammunition […] through national prevention strategies [Action 18(f)];
  • Strengthen efforts to combat, prevent and eradicate the illicit trade in small arms and light weapons [Action 26(g)].

Working Group on Firearms, UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (2025)

[…] strengthen the firearms-related expertise within [the UN], including in relation to the consideration of arms-related early-warning indicators to better support the early warning of conflicts and actions aimed at preventing them [Recommendation 7].

UNIDIR should share information on relevant and appropriate new approaches, technologies and resources related to the use of arms flow data for conflict early warning and support States, upon request, in enhancing prevention and risk mitigation measures [Recommendation 8].

Where arms-related data fits in the early-warning process

Follow the four stages of early warning. Arms-related data is most relevant at the collection and analysis stages.

Arms-related incidents

Click any incident for a fictional scenario in which arms-related data may be collected.

STAGE1

Data collection

What happens

Data is collected from a range of sources: from monitors reporting incidents they observe in their communities or in the field; from scanning news articles; from other open sources, including social media; and/or from governments or civil society partners.

Collected data can be qualitative (e.g., descriptions of events and trends), or quantitative (e.g., numerical figures or statistics).

Where arms-related data fits

Early-warning systems can collect or use many different types of arms-related data. Field monitors, for example, might report rising prices of weapons in the market, which may indicate increased demand and a heightened potential for conflict. Early-warning analysts may obtain governmental data on the number of weapon seizures at a port.

UNIDIR’s Arms-Related Risk Analysis Toolkit sets out a detailed list of arms-related indicators of conflict against which data can be collected. An early-warning system may select indicators depending on (i) its capacity to collect the data, (ii) the type of analysis it undertakes, and/or (iii) the intended final analytical product.

STAGE2

Data analysis

What happens

Once collected, data is processed, including by verification, cleaning, corroboration, weighting, or combination with other sources. Analysts or models then analyze the data for patterns and trends that may indicate whether conflict may break out, persist, or intensify. This may occur at a regional, national, or local level.

Methodological approaches vary and may be used in combination. They include traditional methods (e.g., qualitative expert assessments) and computational methods (e.g., forecasting models based on machine learning that process large volumes of data).

Where arms-related data fits

While the precise causal links have not yet been fully established, research has shown that, in specific circumstances, the proliferation or use of illicit weapons can impact conflict.

Considered together with other data, and contextual factors specific to a region, nation, or locality, arms-related data may provide a useful indication of the risk that conflict may occur, persist, or intensify.

 Stages 3 and 4

Warning and response

Warning

Analysis is communicated to decision-makers: for example, who is at risk, where, how soon, and with what degree of confidence.

Response

Decision-makers act on the warning: preventive diplomacy, mediation, deployment to protect civilians, humanitarian action, or measures aimed at the drivers of violence.

Progress

Click any of the boxes to learn how organizations are using or exploring cutting-edge processes and technologies to aid the incorporation of arms-related data into early-warning systems.

In 2024, the United Nations Institute for Disarmament Research (UNIDIR), Conflict Armament Research (CAR), the Peace Research Institute Oslo (PRIO) and Uppsala University launched the initiative “Building Bridges and Incubating Ideas for Stronger Conflict Prevention: Harnessing Arms and Ammunition Flows Data for Early Warning” to foster the use of arms-related data for more effective early warning.

Since then, numerous initiatives have emerged that are developing, trialing, and deploying innovative ways to collect arms-related data and strengthen its integration into early-warning systems. These efforts respond directly to recommendations identified by the community of practice and summarized in the Pathways to Action report.

Notes and methodology

How these mappings were compiled

The lists of arms-related data sources and key early-warning initiatives were reviewed by participating experts during the 2024 International Conference and finalized by experts from UNIDIR, CAR, PRIO and Uppsala University. They are living, working tools.

Both data sources are primarily based on publicly accessible information. Items in brackets “[]” indicate uncertainty or limited public information and hence are “best estimates” by experts from UNIDIR, CAR, PRIO and Uppsala University, based on available information. Arms and ammunition-related data sources are limited to sources that release databases and regular reports.

Should you identify any inaccurate or incomplete information, or you wish to provide feedback, please contact cap-unidir@un.org. Requests for inclusion of additional sources or initiatives in the mapping are particularly encouraged.
